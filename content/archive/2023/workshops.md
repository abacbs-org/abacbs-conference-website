## W05: Unlocking single cell spatial omics analyses with scdney

**Thursday 7 December 2023, 10:05–17:00, Room 130, ModWest Building (11A), UQ St Lucia.** Organisers: Farhan Ameen, Ellis Patrick, and Shila Ghazanfar (Sydney Uni).

**Summary:** An analytical framework for analysing data from high dimensional spatial omics technologies such as CODEX, CycIF, IMC and High-Definition Spatial Transcriptomics, using scdney — a wrapper package with a suite of Bioconductor packages designed for single cell analysis (simpleSeg, scHot, FuseSOM, scClassify, spicyR, listClust, statial, scFeatures and ClassifyR). By the end of the workshop, attendees were able to implement and assess key steps of a spatial analysis pipeline: cell segmentation, feature normalisation, cell type identification, microenvironment and cell-state characterisation, spatial hypothesis testing and patient classification.

**Agenda:** 10:05am – Data visualisation & cell segmentation; 11:30am – Cell type clustering & classification; 12:30pm – Lunch break; 1:30pm – Downstream spatial analysis & feature generation; 3:30pm – Patient classification; 5:00pm – Workshop conclusion.

## W06: The RUV-III-PRPS R package for effective normalization of RNA-seq data

**Thursday 7 December 2023, 13:00–16:00, Room 415, Chemistry Building (68), UQ St Lucia.** Organiser: Marie Trussart (WEHI).

**Summary:** Large scale datasets generated by different omics technologies present unique challenges in terms of normalization and integration. This workshop focused on the RUV normalization methods for such challenges, presenting the RUVIII-PRPS package — a novel strategy (Molania et al. 2023, *Nat. Biotech.*) using pseudo-replicates of pseudo-samples (PRPS) to normalize RNA-seq data where technical replicates are not available. Participants learned to run the RUVIII-PRPS method and visualise diagnostic plots before and after normalization to assess data quality and consistency.

## W07: Spatial analysis with SPIAT and spaSim to characterise and simulate tissue microenvironments

**Friday 8 December 2023, 09:00–12:30, Room 371, Andrew N. Liveris Building (46), UQ St Lucia.** Organisers: Yuzhou Feng and Anna Trigos (Peter Mac).

**Summary:** Introduced two R packages for the analysis and simulation of spatial proteomics data (multiplex immunohistochemistry, CODEX, MIBI, IMC, etc), published in *Nature Communications* (Feng et al. 2023): SPIAT (SPatial Image Analysis of Tissues), a spatial-platform agnostic toolkit with a suite of spatial analysis algorithms, and spaSim (spatial Simulator), a simulator of tissue spatial patterns. Covered handling spatial proteomics data from reading and formatting through SPIAT's six analysis modules — quality control and visualisation, cell colocalisation, spatial heterogeneity, tumour structure characterisation, and neighbourhood identification — with a hands-on analysis workflow on a real example dataset.

## W09: Transcriptomics for non-model organisms: from sequencing to analyses

**Friday 8 December 2023, 09:00–12:30, Room 230, Andrew N. Liveris Building (46), UQ St Lucia.** Organiser: Dr Jonathan W. Lawley (Griffith University).

**Summary:** RNA-Seq has become a crucial method for comparative transcriptome profiling across both model and non-model organisms. Analysis of RNA-Seq data from non-model organisms presents distinct challenges, often due to the lack of a high-quality reference genome and a relatively scant array of tools for downstream functional analyses. The workshop addressed sequencing alternatives, then worked through a *de novo* assembly, functional annotation, and differential gene expression workflow on the Galaxy Australia platform, discussing limitations, alternatives and integration with other omics data at each step. A free event (not catered); participants brought their own laptop and signed in to Galaxy Australia.
