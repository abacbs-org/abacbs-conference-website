The conference was held in a hybrid setting, meeting and interacting in person in a 'hub' site in each capital city able to host events (Adelaide, Brisbane, Perth and Hobart), with talks video Zoomed live across all states, plus interaction via the Gather.Town platform. International registrations and abstract submissions were welcome. The aim was to share cutting-edge bioinformatics and computational biology research, with an emphasis on how computational research is done.

This year the University of Tasmania was the host, and the conference was joint with The Thirteenth Theoretical Phylogenetics Meeting at UTAS (Phylomania).

- 22 Nov — COMBINE/AYRCOB Student Symposium
- 23-24 Nov — ABACBS conference
- 25 Nov — Phylomania / ABACBS combined talk
- 25-26 Nov — Workshops (ABACBS / Phylomania)

## Overview

The conference was convened by Michael Charleston (University of Tasmania), Chair of the Organising Committee, with Mirana Ramialison and Emily Wong coordinating the Program Committee — see [Committees](/archive/2021/committee/) for the full organising and program committee listing.

The conference featured:

- 6 international keynote speakers
- 6 national keynote speakers
- Oral presentations selected from abstract submissions, lightning talks, and poster sessions (via Gather.Town)
- The COMBINE/AYRCOB Student Symposium (22 November)
- Phylomania, the Thirteenth Theoretical Phylogenetics Meeting, run jointly with ABACBS (25-26 November)
- Workshops (25-26 November): Pathway analysis of multi-omics data using the Reactome database, the Clinical Bioinformatics Symposium, and Viral Metagenomics with Hecatomb
